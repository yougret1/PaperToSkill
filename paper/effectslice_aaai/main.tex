\title{EffectSlice: Run-Level Admission Tests for Paper-Derived Procedural Skills}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{abstract}
Paper-to-agent systems can turn a research paper into an executable skill, but a
shorter skill is not necessarily a faithful or useful one.  We formulate skill
reduction as an \emph{admission decision}: a compact candidate may be accepted
only after the complete paper-derived artifact has demonstrated a positive
effect on a locked task, the candidate is traceable to source-grounded
procedures, and its behavior is preserved on an untouched confirmation family.
EffectSlice is a reducer-agnostic protocol implementing this decision with four
gates: full-artifact eligibility, deterministic source binding, retained-unit
dependency-closure deletion audit, and sealed confirmation with explicit
abstention.  We evaluate
the protocol on two locked implementation tasks derived from SnapATAC2 and
Toolformer, using DeepSeek V3.2 through a trusted vendor API while a commodity
PC performs orchestration, execution, and deterministic scoring.  Both complete
artifacts improve over the no-artifact scaffold on all 21 eligibility cases.
On discovery cases, a 3-of-5-procedure SnapATAC2 slice and a
1-of-5-procedure Toolformer slice each match the complete artifact on 16/16
cases.  Sealed confirmation separates them: the former matches on 59/59 cases
and is admitted, while the latter mismatches on 15/59 and is rejected.  Thus the
case study supplies one task-local admission and one caught false discovery,
not evidence of general cross-paper effectiveness.  It also shows that fewer
procedures need not reduce agent interactions.  The result motivates treating
compactness as a proposal and sealed behavioral evidence as the admission
criterion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Systems that compile papers into agents, code, or reusable skills are making
research artifacts easier to execute \cite{miao2025paper2agent,
zhao2025autoreproduce}.  Execution, however, does not answer a common downstream
question: which parts of the artifact actually preserve its measured benefit
for the task at hand?  Loading every recovered procedure may be unnecessary,
whereas selecting an apparently relevant subset can silently remove a crucial
condition.  This distinction matters for agent skills because textual
compactness, retrieval relevance, and behavioral preservation are different
objects.

Recent systems attack adjacent parts of this problem.  Graph-of-Skills retrieves
dependency-aware bundles \cite{liu2026graphskills}; SkillRAE compiles compact
grounded contexts \cite{meng2026skillrae}; ClawTrace distills skills from
cost-attributed traces \cite{yuan2026clawtrace}; Anything2Skill and SkillFoundry
construct reusable skills from heterogeneous evidence
\cite{pan2026anything2skill,shen2026skillfoundry}.  A large-scale evaluation
framework estimates the marginal utility of whole skills
\cite{shaposhnikov2026agenticskills}.  These advances provide candidates and
scorers, but they do not by themselves establish that (i) the complete artifact
helps on an independently locked task, or (ii) a selected subset preserves that
benefit after selection.

We propose \textbf{EffectSlice}, a protocol that treats a reducer's output as a
hypothesis rather than a result.  Before subset search, the complete artifact
must beat a same-scaffold no-artifact baseline on a frozen eligibility family.
Candidates must consist of deterministic paper-source units.  Selection occurs
on a discovery family; every retained-unit dependency-closure deletion is
audited; then the selected candidate and confirmation family are sealed before
an untouched run.
The outcome is either a task-local admission record or a concrete rejection.
The protocol is reducer-agnostic: retrieval, pruning, tracing, or manual
selection may propose the candidate, but none bypasses the same gate.

Our evaluation is deliberately a falsification-oriented case study rather than
a broad benchmark.  We lock two code-implementation tasks around paper-specific
mechanisms: SnapATAC2's matrix-free spectral embedding
\cite{zhang2024snapatac2} and Toolformer's API-call filtering loss
\cite{schick2023toolformer}.  The result contains both outcomes that an admission
protocol must expose.  A three-procedure slice survives sealed confirmation; a
one-procedure slice that looked perfect during discovery fails 15 confirmation
cases and is rejected.  Reporting the rejection is central evidence for the
gate, not a failed result to be discarded.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate compact paper-derived skill use as an
    \emph{eligibility-conditioned task-local admission decision}, separating
    candidate generation from evidence for accepting the candidate.
    \item We instantiate a traceable protocol combining source-grounded units,
    complete retained-unit dependency-closure deletion audit, immutable family
    hashes, and sealed confirmation with explicit abstention.
    \item We provide an executable two-task case study with one admission and
    one sealed rejection, demonstrating that perfect discovery agreement can
    fail on untouched cases and that procedural compression need not imply
    interaction savings.
\end{itemize}

We do not claim that subset search, delta debugging, provenance, dependency
closure, equivalence testing, or deletion minimality is individually new.  Nor
do we claim cross-paper effectiveness or benefit to ordinary users.  The novel
object is the combined admission decision and its evidence boundary.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Paper reproduction and paper-derived agents.}
PaperBench evaluates agents that recreate research code and execute it in a
fresh environment \cite{starace2025paperbench}; AutoReproduce uses paper lineage
and agents to automate experiment reproduction \cite{zhao2025autoreproduce};
Paper2Agent compiles research papers into interactive agents
\cite{miao2025paper2agent}.  These systems focus on constructing or evaluating
a complete reproduction.  EffectSlice begins after a complete artifact exists
and asks a narrower counterfactual question: does a strict subset preserve the
complete artifact's task-local effect?

\paragraph{Agent skills and compact context.}
SkillFoundry and Anything2Skill construct validated procedural artifacts from
heterogeneous sources \cite{shen2026skillfoundry,pan2026anything2skill}.
Graph-of-Skills preserves prerequisite structure during retrieval
\cite{liu2026graphskills}; SkillRAE compiles selected evidence into compact,
grounded execution context \cite{meng2026skillrae}; and ClawTrace uses
cost-aware traces to preserve, prune, or repair skill content
\cite{yuan2026clawtrace}.  Whole-skill evaluation can measure marginal utility
at scale \cite{shaposhnikov2026agenticskills}.  EffectSlice does not compete as
another reducer.  It supplies a common downstream admission layer for any such
candidate.  Direct matched runs of these reducers remain future work in the
present case study.

\paragraph{Reduction, compression, and equivalence.}
Delta debugging isolates failure-inducing inputs \cite{zeller2002simplifying},
and C-Reduce applies structure-aware reduction to compiler test cases
\cite{regehr2012creduce}.  Cause reduction extends the objective from preserving
a crash to preserving a general observed cause \cite{groce2016cause}.  Prompt
compression methods such as LLMLingua and LLMLingua-2 optimize compactness while
measuring retained task information
\cite{jiang2023llmlingua,pan2024llmlingua2}.  EffectSlice shares the reduction
motif, but conditions reduction on a beneficial complete artifact and separates
adaptive discovery from sealed confirmation.  Its statistical decisions draw
on equivalence testing \cite{wellek2002equivalence}, exact binomial bounds
\cite{clopper1934binomial}, and registered family-wise error control
\cite{holm1979sequential}.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $B$ be a same-scaffold baseline that receives the task but no paper-derived
procedures, and let $F=\{a_1,\ldots,a_K\}$ be the complete artifact represented by
deterministic source-grounded units.  A reducer proposes a strict nonempty
candidate $S\subset F$.  For a locked case $x$, the bounded scorer returns
$y(A,x)\in\{0,1\}$ for artifact condition $A\in\{B,F,S\}$.  Contracts and task
guardrails are hard predicates, not soft score bonuses.

The protocol answers a local question:
\begin{quote}
Given one paper-derived artifact, one task contract, one scorer, and one frozen
case population, may candidate $S$ be admitted as behavior-preserving relative
to an eligible $F$?
\end{quote}
It does not identify a globally minimal core, and an admission for one task says
nothing about another task or paper.

We separate three decisions.  \emph{Eligibility} asks whether $F$ has a positive
casewise effect over $B$.  \emph{Discovery} searches for candidates that agree
with $F$ on a development-isolated case block.  \emph{Confirmation} estimates
preservation and benefit on an untouched block after the candidate and family
have been sealed.  This separation prevents an adaptively selected candidate
from treating the data that selected it as confirmatory evidence.

\section{EffectSlice}

\subsection{Gate 1: Beneficial Full-Artifact Eligibility}

For eligibility cases $x_i$, define
\begin{equation}
e_i = \mathbb{1}\left[y(F,x_i)-y(B,x_i)\geq\delta_{\min}\right].
\end{equation}
The complete artifact proceeds only if the preregistered minimum case count is
met, all contracts and guardrails pass, and the one-sided Clopper--Pearson lower
bound on $\Pr(e_i=1)$ exceeds $p_{\min}$.  Our frozen values are
$\delta_{\min}=0.05$, $p_{\min}=0.8$, $n_E=21$, and $\alpha_E=0.01$.
Failure produces \texttt{no\_validated\_beneficial\_effect}; no slice search is
allowed.  This prevents a reducer from ``preserving'' an artifact that never
helped.

\subsection{Gate 2: Source-Grounded Candidate Registry}

Each unit stores an identifier, paper-derived text span, executable role, and
artifact digest.  A candidate must be a strict nonempty subset, contain only
registered units, satisfy dependency closure, and compile into the same task
interface as $F$.  The general implementation condenses cyclic dependencies
before search.  In the present case study, each five-unit source map is an
acyclic chain, so condensation yields five singleton nodes and its four strict
prefixes are exactly the nonempty dependency-closed strict subsets.  We register
these prefixes in increasing-size order.  This simple reducer is intentional:
the evaluation targets the admission gate, not search quality.

\subsection{Gate 3: Discovery and Dependency-Closure Deletion Audit}

On $n_D=16$ discovery cases, candidates are evaluated in the frozen order until
one has zero casewise mismatches with $F$ and passes the hard contract.  The
selected $S^\star$ is then locked.  For every retained unit $a$, we remove $a$
and cascade the removal of retained units that transitively require it.  The
resulting registered dependency-closed neighbor must fail to preserve $F$ on
the discovery family.  The audit is local to this closure operator and the
registered candidate family: it establishes indispensability under those
neighbors, not atomwise necessity under arbitrary rewrites or global
minimality.

\subsection{Gate 4: Sealed Confirmation}

Before any confirmation provider call, we persist a family record containing
the candidate identifier and digest, source-map digest, case-registry digest,
conditions, thresholds, and evidence boundary.  The confirmation runner refuses
candidate or family mismatches.  Raw result bundles remain immutable.

For $n_C=59$ untouched cases, define a preservation violation and a beneficial
case:
\begin{align}
m_i &= \mathbb{1}[y(S^\star,x_i)\neq y(F,x_i)],\\
b_i &= \mathbb{1}[y(S^\star,x_i)-y(B,x_i)\geq\delta_{\min}].
\end{align}
We report one-sided exact bounds at $\alpha_C=0.02$.  Admission requires (i)
zero observed preservation violations, (ii) an upper bound on the violation
rate no greater than $0.1$, (iii) a lower bound on beneficial prevalence at
least $0.8$, and (iv) all hard contracts and guardrails.  The zero-violation
rule is stricter than merely placing the estimate inside an equivalence band.
The reusable implementation also contains a Holm-corrected registry for larger
hypothesis families; the two empirical decisions reported here use the explicit
casewise gate above.

Any missing score, provider failure after bounded retries, digest mismatch,
contract failure, or insufficient family returns a named abstention or
rejection.  It is never silently removed from a denominator.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Tasks and Conditions}

\paragraph{SNAP-MFSE.}
The task is to implement the paper-specific matrix-free spectral embedding core
used in SnapATAC2 \cite{zhang2024snapatac2}: inverse-document-frequency feature
scaling, row normalization, self-similarity removal and degree computation,
linear-operator exposure, and leading spectral coordinates.  The scorer checks
registered numerical cases, the function contract, and a matrix-free guard.
The selected prefix retains the first three of five units.

\paragraph{TOOLFORMER-FILTER.}
The task is to implement Toolformer's rule for retaining proposed API calls
\cite{schick2023toolformer}: normalized future-token weights, weighted losses,
the stronger counterfactual baseline, thresholding, and ordered application to
all proposals.  The selected prefix retains only weight normalization.  An
initial development task omitted the $t=0$ convention and explicit rejection of
Boolean thresholds.  That bundle is preserved as
\texttt{task\_contract\_failure}; the task contract was corrected before the
eligibility family was run, and the historical bundle is excluded from all
reported gates.

For each task, $B$, $F$, and $S$ use the same prompt, locked workspace, action
interface, case block, and deterministic scorer.  The only intended difference
is the injected artifact.  The action budget is 16.  Development, eligibility,
discovery, and confirmation contain 4, 21, 16, and 59 cases, respectively.

\subsection{API-First Commodity-Device Execution}

We use DeepSeek V3.2 as the primary inference service through a trusted vendor
API; the exact request alias recorded in artifacts is
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}.  A local commodity PC performs orchestration,
bounded tool execution, artifact persistence, scoring, and verification.  No
local accelerator or local language-model deployment is required.  This is the
API-assisted operational meaning of ``ordinary device'' in this study, not a
claim of local model inference.  A GPT-family endpoint is used only for a
development robustness check on the Toolformer task; it does not substitute for
primary-model eligibility or sealed confirmation.

Transient connection, timeout, HTTP 429, and HTTP 5xx failures are retried up to
five times within the same statistical unit.  Exhausted failures count against
the gate.  Provider credentials enter subprocess environments only and are not
persisted in experiment artifacts.

\subsection{Evidence Discipline}

All task prompts, source maps, case registries, candidate registries, family
records, run manifests, transcripts, candidate patches, scores, and derived
summaries are stored with stable paths.  Confirmation families bind upstream
files by SHA-256.  Development failures and superseded protocols remain in the
run directory; summaries select accepted lineages explicitly rather than
overwriting raw results.

\subsection{Both Complete Artifacts Pass Eligibility}

For each task, $B$ fails all 21 eligibility cases and $F$ passes all 21.  The
aggregate casewise effect is 1.0 and the one-sided 99\% exact lower bound on
beneficial prevalence is 0.8031, just above the preregistered 0.8 threshold.
Thus both complete artifacts are eligible for slice discovery.  This result is
only an authorization to search; it is not evidence that a compact candidate
will preserve the effect.

\subsection{Discovery Selects Two Apparently Perfect Slices}

On SNAP-MFSE, the one- and two-unit prefixes are inferior to $F$; the
three-unit prefix is the first candidate to match $F$ on all 16 discovery cases.
Removing any retained unit under dependency closure maps to the baseline or one
of the two inferior prefixes, so all three registered closure-deletion
neighbors fail preservation.  On the Toolformer task, the one-unit prefix
already matches $F$ on 16/16 cases and its only closure-deletion neighbor, $B$,
is inferior.  Both candidates therefore satisfy the same discovery and local
dependency-closure deletion criteria.

\subsection{Sealed Confirmation Separates Admission from False Discovery}

Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:evidence} show the untouched result.
The SNAP-MFSE slice matches $F$ on every confirmation case and remains
beneficial over $B$ on 59/59.  Its preservation-violation upper bound is 0.0642
and beneficial-prevalence lower bound is 0.9358; the matrix-free and interface
guards pass.  The task-local gate admits this candidate.

The Toolformer slice passes only 44/59 confirmation cases, producing 15
preservation violations.  Its upper bound is 0.3905 and beneficial-prevalence
lower bound is 0.6095.  The interface contract passes, but the behavioral gate
rejects the candidate.  Critically, discovery supplied no warning: both selected
candidates had 0/16 mismatches.  The sealed family exposes a false discovery
that a discovery-only evaluation would have reported as successful.

\subsection{Compactness Does Not Imply Lower Execution Cost}

The complete SNAP-MFSE and Toolformer artifacts finish in 7 and 5 API-agent
actions, whereas both selected slices use all 16 actions.  The baseline also
uses 16.  We therefore make no time-, token-, or cost-saving claim.  The
SNAP-MFSE result supports behavioral preservation for a smaller procedural
artifact, not operational efficiency.  This distinction is a second reason to
place compact candidates behind an effect gate rather than treating artifact
length as utility.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the case study establishes.}
The evidence supports two task-local statements: one registered slice preserved
its complete artifact on the locked confirmation family, and one seemingly
perfect discovery candidate did not.  Together they demonstrate that the
protocol can both admit and withhold.  The negative Toolformer result is
especially informative because it directly tests the value of sealing: without
confirmation, the selected one-unit slice would have been a false success.

\paragraph{What remains unestablished.}
Two tasks cannot estimate a cross-paper admission rate, compare reducers, or
show that EffectSlice generally finds useful cores.  Our simple ordered-prefix
reducer is not a baseline against Graph-of-Skills, ClawTrace, or SkillRAE.
Likewise, the study does not show that nonexpert users reproduce papers faster
or more accurately.  Those are separate empirical questions requiring a frozen
paper-task population, direct matched reducer outputs, and a user study with
time-to-correct-use, error adoption, and cognitive-load outcomes.

\paragraph{Why API-first execution is the correct hardware boundary.}
The research question concerns artifact admission, not local language-model
serving.  Requiring a local quantized model would add a model-capability and
quantization confound unrelated to the gate.  We therefore keep model inference
at a trusted vendor endpoint and require only that local orchestration and
verification run on an ordinary PC.  Vendor dependence remains a reproducibility
threat and should be tested through additional endpoints, but an 80GB-class GPU
is not part of the method.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{External validity.}
The case study contains two code-generation tasks, one model family for sealed
evidence, and two source papers.  Both task contracts reduce paper mechanisms to
bounded implementation oracles rather than reproducing the papers' complete
training or biological analyses.  No general EffectSlice claim is warranted.

\paragraph{Statistical unit.}
The 21, 16, and 59 counts refer to locked deterministic test cases evaluated
against one generated candidate per condition; they are not 21, 16, or 59
independent language-model generations.  Exact binomial bounds are meaningful
relative to the preregistered case generator and its sampling assumptions, not
as confidence intervals over models, prompts, papers, or users.  Correlated or
unrepresentative cases can make those bounds optimistic.  Future evaluation
must add independent agent runs and genuinely held-out paper-task pairs.

\paragraph{Specification sensitivity.}
The excluded Toolformer development bundle shows that a missing $t=0$
convention and Boolean-threshold rule can dominate scores.  Source grounding
does not eliminate ambiguity in translating prose into a task contract.  We
preserve this failure and freeze the corrected task before eligibility, but
independent contract review remains necessary.

\paragraph{Selection and baseline coverage.}
The ordered-prefix registry exercises the gate but not a complex reducer or
dependency graph.  Direct outputs from Graph-of-Skills, ClawTrace, and SkillRAE
were not run under matched budgets.  The current evidence therefore supports a
protocol case study, not comparative superiority.

\paragraph{Efficiency and user benefit.}
Neither admitted nor rejected slices reduce API-agent actions; input and output
tokens are also not lower in the sealed runs.  No human participants were
studied.  We explicitly exclude savings, usability, and ``helping ordinary
people'' from the empirical claims.

\section{Broader Impact}

Admission gates can reduce overconfident adoption of compact paper-derived
instructions by making rejection an expected output and retaining negative
evidence.  They can also create false assurance if users generalize a local
certificate beyond its task, case generator, provider, or scorer.  Every
certificate should therefore expose those boundaries and the raw evidence
needed for audit.  The method executes generated code only in bounded workspaces
and keeps credentials out of persisted artifacts; these controls reduce but do
not remove software supply-chain and provider risks.

\section{Conclusion}

EffectSlice reframes reduction of paper-derived skills as a sealed task-local
admission problem.  A reducer may propose a compact artifact, but eligibility of
the complete artifact, source binding, dependency-closure deletion evidence,
and untouched confirmation determine whether it may be used as a behavioral
substitute.  In
two locked tasks, discovery alone selects two perfect-looking candidates;
confirmation admits one and rejects the other.  The result is intentionally
narrow, yet it captures the protocol's central purpose: compactness is not
evidence, and a credible workflow must be able to say no.
