\section{Problem Formulation}

Let a paper $P$ describe a bounded procedure that an LLM agent must reuse for a task $t$. An upstream extractor produces a \emph{full specification} $F$, and an upstream reducer produces a shorter \emph{candidate specification} $S$. Neither artifact is treated as an oracle: $F$ may omit an operational requirement, while $S$ may remove information needed by $t$. We ask a local substitution question under a fixed evaluation boundary $\Omega$: can $S$ replace $F$ for $(t,\Omega)$? A no-specification baseline $B$ completes the comparison set $C\in\{B,F,S\}$. It reveals tasks that the model can solve from the scaffold alone, whereas $F$ tests whether the reference specification and evaluation package are sufficiently informative.

SkillAudit makes the candidate boundary inspectable by representing the paper procedure as source-addressed atoms $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_K\}$ and a directed dependency graph $G=(A,E)$. Each atom is a typed, executable unit
\begin{equation}
a_i=(\mathrm{id}_i,\ell_i,u_i,r_i,D_i),
\label{eq:atom}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_i$ is an exact source locator, $u_i$ is the model-facing instruction, $r_i$ is its procedural role, and $D_i$ contains direct prerequisites. A retained set $A_S\subsetneq A$ is structurally admissible only if it is nonempty, acyclic, and dependency closed. A deterministic renderer $R$ then produces $F=R(A)$ and $S=R(A_S)$. This representation identifies what was retained and removed; it does not by itself establish behavioral sufficiency.
The dependency edges are induced by the prerequisite sets, $E=\{(a_j,a_i):a_j\in D_i\}$. Dependency closure therefore requires $a_i\in A_S\Rightarrow D_i\subseteq A_S$.

For registered block $r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and condition $C$, a fresh agent session yields a validity bit $v_{C,r}$, contract-pass bit $c_{C,r}$, patch-present bit $p_{C,r}$, and private score $q_{C,r}\in[0,1]$. Here $c_{C,r}=1$ means that every registered hard contract passed. Given a prespecified score threshold $\tau$, operational success is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_{C,r}&=\mathbb{1}\!\left[v_{C,r}=1\land c_{C,r}=1\land p_{C,r}=1\right.\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{45mm}\left.{}\land q_{C,r}\ge\tau\right],\\
k_C&=\sum_{r=1}^{n}z_{C,r}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:success}
\end{equation}
Private cases evaluate one submitted patch and are not independent agent runs. Likewise, two tasks derived from one paper are related observations; our cross-paper inference therefore aggregates tasks within a paper and treats the paper as the resampling unit.

\section{SkillAudit}

SkillAudit is a post-compression validator rather than a reducer. It receives a fixed candidate, binds that candidate to its paper and task, executes matched $B/F/S$ blocks, validates the resulting evidence, and emits a bounded decision with a field-level rationale. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} separates upstream candidate construction from SkillAudit's validation path. Passing source and closure checks establishes an inspectable, structurally well-formed boundary; it does not establish behavioral sufficiency. A compliant prospective audit seals the candidate, $\Omega$, and $\gamma$ before held-out execution begins. In this study, the $B/F/S$ executions are fresh, whereas the pooled six-state policy was constructed afterward and is therefore reported only as a retrospective diagnostic replay.

\subsection{Source Binding and Candidate Construction}

The evaluation-package builder registers atoms from quoted paper passages. Document digests and page, line, or byte spans identify the source; role labels distinguish operations, parameters, guards, ordering constraints, and output contracts. Dependencies are explicit because a prerequisite may occur in another paragraph, caption, or equation. Before rendering, the closure auditor rejects unknown identifiers, cycles, a non-strict candidate, and retained atoms whose prerequisites are absent. A fixed topological order with atom-identifier tie breaking makes repeated rendering byte stable.

Structural validity and behavioral sufficiency answer different questions. Keeping a solver instruction while dropping its preprocessing prerequisite violates closure before any model call. Dropping the solver together with its dependent steps can remain structurally valid while making the candidate behaviorally insufficient. SkillAudit preserves this distinction: the graph explains the candidate boundary, while held-out execution tests whether that boundary is adequate for the registered task.

\subsection{Frozen Evaluation Boundary}

The evaluation boundary contains every factor that can change the interpretation of a comparison:
\begin{equation}
\Omega=(P,A,G,B,F,S,t,W,M,T,U,Q,\sigma,\tau,\gamma),
\label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the starter workspace, $M$ the model and decoding configuration, $T$ the tool interface, $U$ the action budget, $Q$ the public tests, private cases, and scorer, $\sigma$ the blocked condition schedule, and $\gamma$ the decision policy. Cryptographic digests bind each concrete file to these symbols. Every condition begins from an isolated copy of $W$ and a fresh conversation, and the registered schedule balances condition order and private-registry assignment.

Public tests provide visible development feedback, but the private scorer is terminal and one-way. Once the agent submits, its patch is frozen and scored without returning private results to the session. Case-generation seeds, registry assignment, schedule order, prompts, and model identifiers are recorded. When the executor does not expose an enforceable sampling seed, the evidence record distinguishes deterministic orchestration from nondeterministic model execution instead of claiming backend determinism.

\subsection{Matched Execution and Evidence Validity}

SkillAudit interprets validity before performance. A completed submission that violates a hard contract or scores below $\tau$ is a valid experimental failure and contributes $z_{C,r}=0$ to the registered denominator. A row is invalid only when the registered execution cannot be interpreted, for example because a required digest or terminal output is absent or the scorer does not produce a registered result. Thus missing execution evidence cannot silently become evidence against $S$.

Let $V=1$ only when every registered row and schedule-level integrity check is complete. Because $F$ and $S$ are matched within a block, SkillAudit retains the paired differences $d^z_r=z_{S,r}-z_{F,r}$ and $d^q_r=q_{S,r}-q_{F,r}$. Denote their interval bounds by $[L_z,U_z]$ and $[L_q,U_q]$. These paired quantities test candidate noninferiority; marginal totals alone cannot reveal whether $F$ and $S$ succeeded on different blocks.

\subsection{Evidence-Aware Decision}

The policy $\gamma$ specifies a baseline ceiling $b_{\max}$, minimum full and candidate counts $f_{\min}$ and $s_{\min}$, and noninferiority margins $\epsilon_z$ and $\epsilon_q$. Let $g_B=[k_B\le b_{\max}]$, $g_F=[k_F\ge f_{\min}]$, and $g_S=[k_S\ge s_{\min}]$. Paired noninferiority is $N=[L_z\ge-\epsilon_z\land L_q\ge-\epsilon_q]$, while clear inferiority is $R=[U_z<-\epsilon_z\lor U_q<-\epsilon_q]$. SkillAudit applies the ordered rule
\begin{equation}
\delta_{t,\Omega}(S)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{Invalid}, & V=0,\\
\mathsf{I_B}, & V\land\neg g_B,\\
\mathsf{I_F}, & V\land g_B\land\neg g_F,\\
\mathsf{R_C}, & V\land g_B\land g_F\land(\neg g_S\lor R),\\
\mathsf{Accept}, & V\land g_B\land g_F\land g_S\land N,\\
\mathsf{I_E}, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathsf{I_B}$, $\mathsf{I_F}$, $\mathsf{R_C}$, and $\mathsf{I_E}$ denote baseline-inconclusive, full-inconclusive, reject-candidate, and evidence-inconclusive. The ordering matters: a strong baseline makes the task nondiagnostic; an insufficient $F$ invalidates the reference comparison; and a wide paired interval withholds admission without asserting inferiority. Only \textsc{Accept} authorizes substitution, and only for $(t,\Omega)$.

The numerical policy is application-level: its thresholds, margins, interval construction, and minimum block count must be calibrated and sealed before an audit used for admission. Our primary experiment evaluates the matched endpoints rather than instantiate a confirmatory admission policy. For transparency, the supplementary material gives the exact values used in a separately labeled retrospective replay.

\subsection{Auditable Evidence Record}

The output is not only a label. SkillAudit emits
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}=(d_P,d_A,d_F,d_S,d_{\Omega},\mathcal{O},\mathcal{C},\delta,\rho),
\label{eq:evidence}
\end{equation}
where the digest terms identify the paper, registry, rendered artifacts, and boundary; $\mathcal{O}$ contains the retained requests, trajectories, patches, and scores; $\mathcal{C}$ contains field-level integrity and decision checks; and $\rho$ is a human-readable rationale. The record declares its retained evidence scope and cannot claim provenance beyond that scope. Accepted candidates can therefore be distributed with the exact task and evidence that authorize their use. Rejected, inconclusive, and invalid candidates retain a concrete repair path rather than an undifferentiated failure label.
