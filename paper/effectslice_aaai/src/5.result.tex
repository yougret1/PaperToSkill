\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Questions and Design}

The experiments separate utility, local substitutability, and audit validity. \textbf{RQ1} asks whether the full and simplified paper-derived specifications improve task outcomes over a no-specification baseline. \textbf{RQ2} asks whether $S$ is distinguishable from $F$ in aggregate and whether the answer changes across executions, papers, or domains. \textbf{RQ3} asks whether the registered decision logic and model-facing evaluation distinguish known-truth preserving and destructive transformations. Finally, offline component ablations and bounded reducer, structural, and model comparisons probe which observations generalize beyond the primary construction. This organization follows evidence function rather than run chronology.

\paragraph{Cross-paper benchmark.}
The benchmark contains \PrimaryPapers{} papers, two tasks per paper, and four mechanism domains: NLP, software engineering, data analysis, and agent/tool use. The mechanisms are LLMLingua, LLMLingua-2, context-aware sentence compression, delta debugging, C-Reduce, Perses, HDBSCAN, Leiden, SnapATAC2, ReAct, Reflexion, and Toolformer \cite{jiang2023llmlingua,pan2024llmlingua2,liskavets2025contextcompression,zeller2002simplifying,regehr2012creduce,sun2018perses,campello2015hdbscan,traag2019leiden,zhang2024snapatac2,yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion,schick2023toolformer}. Each paper-task has a full specification, a dependency-closed strict-subset candidate, a starter workspace, public tests, two private registries, and hard output contracts. The registered primary family \texttt{S\_dag\_ratio\_60\_v1} applies the same deterministic DAG-ratio rule to every task: $F$ contains 12 atoms and $S$ contains 8, retaining 56.1--59.8\% of specification tokens (58.0\% pooled, a 42.0\% reduction). The evaluation-package builder constructs the atoms, dependencies, tasks, and scorers from the cited methods; Section~\ref{sec:limitations} treats this shared construction process as a validity limitation.

\paragraph{Primary execution.}
We perform three fresh schedule-matched repetitions (SOL1--SOL3), each with six balanced $B/F/S$ blocks per paper-task. The homogeneous primary view contains \PrimaryRows{} valid terminal rows: \PrimaryTasks{} tasks $\times$ \PrimaryRepeats{} repetitions $\times$ \PrimaryBlocksPerRepeat{} blocks $\times$ three conditions. Every response identifies the executor as \texttt{gpt-5.6-sol}; requests use temperature 0, top-$p$ 1.0, at most 8,192 output tokens, and the registered private-score threshold $\tau=0.99$. Each condition receives a fresh workspace and conversation, private feedback is terminal, and the registered schedules fix condition order and registry assignment. Because the executor exposes no enforceable sampling seed, the three repetitions are independent executions rather than controlled random-seed trials.

\paragraph{Endpoints and statistical units.}
The binary endpoint is operational success from Equation~\ref{eq:success}; the continuous endpoint is private score. Contrasts are paired within registered blocks. For cross-paper summaries, we aggregate both tasks and all repetitions within each paper, then draw 10,000 fixed-seed percentile-bootstrap samples over the \PrimaryPapers{} papers. These intervals describe sensitivity within the registered benchmark, not uncertainty over an unlimited population of papers. Private cases jointly score one patch and are not independent observations. A completed response that fails a hard contract remains a valid zero; a completed response alone is not task success.

\paragraph{Controls and auxiliary suites.}
Controls-v2 crosses four tasks, two registries, four arms, and three independent executions, yielding \ControlRows{} valid model rows plus \ControlDeterministicCells{} deterministic known-truth fixtures. The four model arms are the full reference, an identity rendering, removal of registered redundancy, and removal of an exact-output contract. Offline component replay removes individual deterministic gates without new model calls. Separate four-anchor suites compare alternate reducers, a three-level structural ladder, and six model slots. Because those suites reuse paper-task observations, differ in repetition depth, or alter candidate content, we report them as descriptive robustness evidence rather than independent causal effects or a model leaderboard.

\subsection{RQ2: Aggregate Similarity Masks Local Heterogeneity}

The $S-F$ contrast is much smaller than either baseline contrast. Its operational-success effect is \SFSuccessEffect{} [\SFSuccessCILow{}, \SFSuccessCIHigh{}], and its private-score effect is \SFScoreEffect{} [\SFScoreCILow{}, \SFScoreCIHigh{}]. Both intervals cross zero. The pooled analysis therefore detects no aggregate difference between $S$ and $F$; it does not establish superiority, equivalence, or task-level admissibility.

Figure~\ref{fig:primary-effects}(a) shows paper-level effects ranging from negative values to gains above 0.8. The largest improvements occur for Perses, C-Reduce, Reflexion, LLMLingua, ReAct, and Leiden, whereas HDBSCAN remains difficult even with paper-derived information. At the domain level, $F-B$ and $S-B$ are positive for both endpoints in all four domains. Software engineering has the largest effects; data analysis has the smallest operational gains despite positive score gains. The $S-F$ direction is positive in NLP and software engineering but negative in data analysis and agent/tool use. RQ2 therefore has a bounded answer: the aggregate analysis does not resolve a difference between $S$ and $F$, while their ordering remains paper- and domain-dependent.

The controls also expose the boundary of this result. Identity has three discordant pairs, so representation-preserving changes do not make stochastic trajectories row-wise invariant; redundancy deletion changes one matched outcome and supports no directional benefit. Terminal outcomes agree across all three executions for \ControlStableUnits{}/\ControlTotalUnits{} task--registry--arm units, and the fluctuating units remain in the denominator. RQ3 supports deterministic label mechanics and sensitivity to one destructive contract family, but not broad false-admit or false-reject rates.

\subsection{Component and Generality Checks}

The retrospective six-state replay admits no natural task: among \PrimaryTasks{} paper-task units it yields \SolStrictAccept{} \textsc{Accept}, \SolBaselineInconclusive{} baseline-inconclusive, \SolFullInconclusive{} full-inconclusive, and \SolEvidenceInconclusive{} evidence-inconclusive states, with no reject-candidate or invalid states. This distribution exposes task and package limitations: seven scaffolds are already too capable, 14 full references miss the diagnostic minimum, and three paired intervals remain unresolved. It is therefore diagnostic evidence about where the current registry cannot support substitution, not validation of admission accuracy.

Offline ablation shows why the audit is more than a marginal $F/S$ comparison. Removing $B$ changes \AblationBChangedTasks{}/\PrimaryTasks{} retrospective task states. On the known-truth fixtures, binary contract-only and digest-only gates each recover \AblationBinaryContractCorrect{}/\AblationTotalFixtures{} states, whereas contract tri-state and the full integrity-aware gate recover \AblationTriStateCorrect{}/\AblationTotalFixtures{}. Thus the baseline changes diagnosis, and the distinction between failed and missing contract evidence is necessary on the registered fixture suite. Because the natural tasks lack known admission truth and the pooled policy is retrospective, these are component-sensitivity results rather than calibrated accuracy estimates.

The supplementary checks reinforce this cautious interpretation. On four Sol anchors, alternate reducers each succeed in \ReducerDAGSuccess{}/\ReducerCandidateRows{} rows versus \ReducerFSuccess{}/\ReducerFRows{} separately paired $F$ rows, while ladder success changes from \LadderLZeroSuccess{}/\LadderCandidateRows{} and \LadderLOneSuccess{}/\LadderCandidateRows{} to \LadderLTwoSuccess{}/\LadderCandidateRows{}. Across \RobustnessModelSlots{} model slots, $F-B$ and $S-B$ remain positive but $S-F$ changes sign. On the four shared anchors, Sol $S$ uses \SolSTokenReduction{} fewer executor-reported total tokens per request and has \SolSLatencyReduction{} lower median wall time than $F$. Shared anchors, fixed-scaffold tokens, and unequal repetitions make these descriptive robustness and system-cost results, not causal reducer effects or a model leaderboard.
