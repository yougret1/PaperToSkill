\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Traceability and sufficiency are complementary.}
Source locators and dependency closure make the candidate boundary inspectable, but neither proves behavioral sufficiency. Conversely, a private score without a source map cannot explain what paper content was retained. SkillAudit connects these views by testing the mapped boundary through matched $B/F/S$ execution.

\paragraph{The baseline and controls answer different validity questions.}
The no-specification condition succeeds in \PrimaryBSuccess{}/\PrimaryRowsPerCondition{} executions, and adding $B$ changes \AblationBChangedTasks{}/\PrimaryTasks{} retrospective task states; $B$ therefore identifies tasks whose scaffold is already sufficient. Known-truth fixtures instead validate the implemented decision logic, while the destructive arm checks sensitivity to one contract family. Neither result independently verifies the author-constructed atoms, tasks, or scorers, so we do not convert control behavior into population sensitivity or specificity.

\paragraph{Candidate quality is local, and inconclusive states preserve action.}
The aggregate Sol $S-F$ intervals cross zero, but paper, domain, reducer, and model-setting orderings vary. Aggregate similarity therefore cannot authorize every substitution. SkillAudit preserves the reason: baseline-inconclusive calls for a more diagnostic task, full-inconclusive for repair of $F$ or the evaluation package, rejection for candidate revision, and evidence-inconclusive for stronger matched evidence.

\paragraph{Evidence records bound reuse.}
An accepted candidate is not a task-independent ``core'' of a paper; it substitutes for $F$ only under one $(t,\Omega)$. Reuse should therefore carry the paper digest, atom boundary, model, task, workspace, scorer, registry, schedule, thresholds, terminal ledger, and rationale. Changing any field creates a new claim that requires a new audit.

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:limitations}

The benchmark contains \PrimaryPapers{} papers and two tasks per paper across four domains, but remains a finite, constructed registry. Paper-bootstrap intervals measure sensitivity within this registry, not uncertainty over all scientific papers. One evaluation-package builder defines atoms, dependencies, tasks, private tests, and $F$; independent expert construction, blinded scorer review, and agreement measurement are needed before treating the registry as general paper-fidelity ground truth.

The six-state policy is prospective in form, but its thresholds are not calibrated to population false-admit risk and its pooled replay was constructed after the Sol registration. Controls-v2 covers four tasks and one destructive contract family; component replay has no independent truth; reducer, ladder, and model suites share four anchors and differ in execution depth. These analyses support bounded sensitivity and robustness, not confirmatory admission, causal component effects, or general cross-model rankings.

The executor exposes no enforceable sampling seed. Deterministic orchestration and analysis seeds are recorded, and aggregate baseline contrasts persist across three fresh calls, but individual outcomes vary. External bundles change both model and execution configuration, and request-token counts include the fixed scaffold. The reported analyses are scoped to the retained 2,688-row terminal ledger; the artifact does not support attempt-level reconstruction beyond this declared scope.

No human participants were studied, so we make no claims about researcher comprehension or reproduction speed. SkillAudit may reduce overconfident reuse by exposing decision boundaries, but detaching \textsc{Accept} from its paper-task and scorer can create false assurance. Paper-derived code also retains execution and supply-chain risks; releases should preserve inconclusive, rejected, and invalid records rather than publish only favorable candidates.

\section{Conclusion}

SkillAudit combines source-addressed atoms, dependency closure, matched no-specification/full/candidate execution, evidence validity, diagnostic states, and a replayable record for task-local substitution audits. Across three GPT-5.6 Sol repetitions, both paper-derived artifacts improve operational success and private score over $B$ on \PrimaryPapers{} papers and \PrimaryTasks{} paper-task units, while $S-F$ remains near zero in aggregate and heterogeneous locally. Known-truth controls recover every deterministic label and detect removal of a registered exact-output contract. The evidence supports traceable local auditing, not task-independent equivalence, universal admissibility, or independently calibrated registry validity.
